# The Virgin of the Rocks — Paris & London

Leonardo da Vinci’s *The Virgin of the Rocks* exists in two closely related versions: one in the Musée du Louvre and one in the National Gallery, London. Both show the Virgin Mary, the infant Christ, the young John the Baptist, and an angel gathered inside a strange rocky landscape. The scene feels less like a conventional room than the beginning of the world.

The Paris version is darker and more mysterious, with the figures emerging from the grotto almost as if they belong to it. The London version opens the composition into clearer light and makes some of the forms easier to read. Yet the differences are subtle: a hand, a face, a colour, a shift in emphasis. Leonardo repeats the image without making it feel entirely the same.

Look between the two versions rather than deciding which is “better.” Notice how the cave changes the mood, how the children’s gestures organize the group, and how the angel’s presence remains both gentle and difficult to explain. Is this a shelter, a birthplace, a vision, or a world before history? Let the two paintings answer each other.
