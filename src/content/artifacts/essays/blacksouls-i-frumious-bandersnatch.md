# Frumious Bandersnatch

Frumious Bandersnatch moves from guardian and riddle-maker to a creature whose friendship can become a covenant. The complete source gallery makes the route’s abrupt changes in tone—comic, threatening, and cosmic—visible.

> **Content warning:** Adults-only content warning: Bandersnatch’s source material includes sexual content, coercion, dungeon violence, and dark-route imagery. This page preserves route context without explicit prose.

## Appearances | BS1

In *BLACKSOULS I*, Bandersnatch is a relatively minor NPC who guards the entrance to Alice’s Library in the Finished Tea Party. Her riddle is the peaceful route through the encounter; getting it wrong or attacking her starts a difficult fight.

## At the entrance

The guard, riddle, friendly, attack, protest, and fight frames introduce Bandersnatch as a gatekeeper who can be approached in more than one register.

## Appearances | BS2

Bandersnatch returns in *BLACKSOULS II* in a more humanoid form. Her lair is reached through the foggy, rail-bridge section of the Fuming Forest. After defeating her, Grimm can enter the dwelling, speak with her, and form a covenant that improves attack power and counterattack chances.

## Personality

Bandersnatch is frantic in both games, but that quality becomes more pronounced in the sequel’s humanoid version. She is aggressive and violent while also being cute, passionate, simple-minded, and naïve. Her attachment to Alice gives that energy a more vulnerable side.

Although she looks dangerous, Bandersnatch’s face is notably cute, and her tan is part of what makes her attractive. The source also gives that attraction a small character beat: she becomes angry when Robin Hood says that he does not like dark-skinned girls.

## Design

In the first game, Bandersnatch appears as a lion-like creature that may also be read as a griffin; her battle form is a much larger purple monster. The sequel reworks her into a more humanoid design, preserving the frantic movement and creature-like intensity while making her expressions and route interactions more direct.
