# BLACKSOULS II: Choosing an Alice

> **Spoiler warning:** This essay describes the opening choice and the broad shape of the prologue. It does not cover later routes, endings, or the sequel's deeper reveals.

If the prologue is allowed to play, *BLACKSOULS II* greets the player with an Alice of their choosing. The choice is not between unrelated characters but between three family roles: daughter, little sister, or mother. Each version gives the opening its own emotional emphasis and its own early scenes.

The apparent stability of that beginning does not last. The world quickly starts to collapse, and the player is carried into a mysterious area without anyone dependable to lean on. The transition is important because it turns the Alice choice from a gentle introduction into a threshold: the player has chosen the relationship that frames the first moments, but not the world that waits beyond them.

From there, the journey opens into Wonderland. This is not a cheerful destination or a simple retelling. It is a dangerous fantasy space where isolation, discovery, and threat arrive together. Familiar names and images remain, but *BLACKSOULS II* keeps asking what happens when a storybook world has already been damaged.

The Alice opening is a small version of the sequel's larger design. It begins with recognition, asks for a personal choice, and then removes the safety of the introduction. The player is left to explore a world whose characters may become companions, obstacles, victims, or something harder to name.

That is why the prologue deserves its own essay. It establishes the emotional logic of *BLACKSOULS II* before the map becomes large: choose carefully, grow attached quickly, and do not assume that a familiar fairy tale is offering protection.
