# Oni (2001): Bungie's Forgotten Cyberpunk Experiment

![Oni title screen](/media/games/oni/oni-title-screen.webp)

When people talk about Bungie, the conversation usually starts with *Halo* and sometimes drifts back to *Marathon*. But buried between those two eras is one of the strangest, most ambitious games the studio ever published: *Oni*.

Released in 2001 and developed by Bungie West, *Oni* mixed anime-inspired storytelling, close-quarters martial arts combat, gunplay, cyberpunk worldbuilding, and cinematic action into something that still feels unusually bold even today.

It was messy. It was experimental. It was years ahead of its time in several ways.

And despite its age, *Oni* is still absolutely worth playing.

---

## What Is Oni?

*Oni* is a third-person action game set in a dystopian future where massive corporations and authoritarian organizations control society under the excuse of preserving order.

You play as Konoko, an elite operative working for the Technological Crimes Task Force (TCTF). At first, she appears to be a loyal agent fighting terrorism and organized crime. But as the story unfolds, Konoko discovers conspiracies, corruption, and secrets about her own identity that force her to question everything she believes.

The game was released for:

- PC (Windows and Mac)
- PlayStation 2

At the time, the industry was flooded with military shooters and early 3D action games. *Oni* stood out immediately because it leaned heavily into anime and Hong Kong action cinema influences.

You can feel inspirations from:

- *Ghost in the Shell*
- *Akira*
- *Bubblegum Crisis*
- John Woo gun-fu films
- Late 90s cyberpunk anime aesthetics

The result is a game with a distinct identity that still feels recognizable more than two decades later.

---

## The Combat Was Way Ahead of Its Time

![Konoko in combat gear](/media/games/oni/oni-pose.jpeg)

The biggest reason people still remember *Oni* is the combat system.

At first glance, it looks like a standard third-person shooter from the early 2000s.

It is not.

Instead of relying purely on guns, *Oni* combines:

- Martial arts combos
- Dodges and rolls
- Throws and counters
- Context-sensitive attacks
- Firearms with limited ammo
- Fast movement and aggressive positioning

In many modern action games, melee combat and gunplay feel separated into different systems. *Oni* tried to merge them into one fluid combat loop long before that became common.

You might enter a room shooting enemies with an assault rifle, run out of ammo, dive sideways to avoid gunfire, disarm an enemy, then finish the fight with spinning kicks and throws.

That flow still feels surprisingly modern.

### Konoko Feels Athletic

One thing *Oni* gets incredibly right is character movement.

Konoko doesn’t move like a heavy tank. She sprints, flips, slides, and pivots with speed that makes the game feel energetic even today.

A lot of early 3D action games feel stiff when revisited. *Oni* feels fast.

The animation work deserves enormous credit here:

- Combat transitions are smooth
- Kicks have impact
- Enemy reactions feel physical
- Movement animations communicate momentum well

Even now, the hand-to-hand combat has a unique rhythm that many games never replicated.

---

## The Anime Influence Gives It Personality

Most Western games in 2001 were still hesitant to fully embrace anime-inspired presentation.

*Oni* did the opposite.

Everything about the game leans into that style:

- Sharp angular character designs
- Stylized facial art
- Futuristic armor and weapons
- Cyberpunk architecture
- Dramatic story beats
- Emotional character arcs
- High-energy action choreography

The game even uses animated story sequences that resemble anime production storyboards.

This gives *Oni* a personality that helps it stand apart from other games of its era.

Even visually, the game has aged more gracefully than many realistic games from the same period because the stylization carries it.

---

## The Story Is Darker Than It First Appears

At the beginning, *Oni* presents itself as a straightforward action game.

But underneath the combat is a surprisingly bleak science-fiction story.

Themes include:

- Government corruption
- Human experimentation
- Identity and memory
- Corporate power
- Environmental collapse
- Psychological trauma

Konoko’s journey slowly transforms from a police-action narrative into something more tragic and introspective.

The game’s world feels unstable and morally compromised. Nobody seems fully trustworthy, and the line between protecting society and controlling it becomes increasingly blurred.

That atmosphere is a major reason *Oni* still resonates with players who enjoy cyberpunk fiction.

---

## Why Oni Was So Unique

Part of what makes *Oni* fascinating is how difficult it is to categorize.

It wasn’t fully:

- A shooter
- A fighting game
- A stealth game
- A character-action game
- An RPG

But it borrowed ideas from all of them.

In hindsight, *Oni* feels like an early prototype for many action design trends that became popular years later.

You can see traces of systems that would later appear in:

- *Max Payne*
- *Mirror’s Edge*
- *Devil May Cry*
- *Vanquish*
- *Remember Me*
- *Binary Domain*
- Modern cinematic action games

That experimental energy makes the game feel important historically, even if it never became a massive commercial phenomenon.

---

## The Soundtrack and Atmosphere Still Work

*Oni* also nails atmosphere.

The soundtrack blends electronic, industrial, and ambient sounds that reinforce the game’s lonely futuristic world.

Combined with:

- sterile laboratories
- industrial compounds
- rain-soaked urban environments
- corporate facilities
- underground hideouts

…the game creates a cyberpunk tone that still feels effective.

There is a constant sense of tension and decay.

The world feels like it is quietly collapsing beneath the surface.

---

## Yes, It Has Flaws

To be fair, *Oni* is not a perfect game.

Modern players will absolutely notice some rough edges:

- Camera problems during fights
- Repetitive environments
- Occasional awkward AI behavior
- Limited save systems in some versions
- Platforming sections that can feel frustrating

Some mechanics also feel underexplained by modern standards.

But the important thing is this:

The game’s strengths are strong enough to outweigh its age.

A lot of older games become difficult to revisit because they no longer feel interesting.

*Oni* still feels interesting.

That matters.

---

## The Community Kept It Alive

Another reason *Oni* survives today is its dedicated fan community.

Over the years, fans created:

- Patches
- HD texture improvements
- Modding tools
- Restored content
- Gameplay tweaks
- Community launchers

The modern community edition makes the game significantly easier to run on current systems.

That means new players can experience *Oni* without fighting old compatibility problems as much as they would with many other early 2000s PC games.

---

## Why Oni Is Worth Playing Today

So why revisit *Oni* in 2026?

Because very few games feel like it.

Even now, its combination of:

- anime cyberpunk storytelling
- martial arts combat
- third-person shooting
- fast movement
- dystopian atmosphere
- experimental action design

still feels distinctive.

Modern games are often highly polished but carefully designed around proven formulas.

*Oni* feels risky.

You can feel developers trying ideas because they were excited about them, not because market research demanded them.

That creative ambition gives the game lasting value.

And if you enjoy:

- cyberpunk fiction
- anime aesthetics
- experimental action games
- early 2000s PC classics
- Bungie history
- stylish combat systems

then *Oni* is absolutely worth experiencing.

---

## Final Thoughts

*Oni* is one of those games that never became a giant franchise, yet still built a loyal following because it attempted something genuinely different.

It captured a specific moment in gaming history:

- when studios experimented more freely,
- when genre boundaries were less rigid,
- and when ambitious ideas sometimes mattered more than technical perfection.

More than twenty years later, *Oni* remains a fascinating blend of anime, cyberpunk, martial arts, and sci-fi action.

It may not be flawless.

But it is memorable.

And sometimes that matters more.
