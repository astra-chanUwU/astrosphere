# Sonic Riders: The Racing Game I Never Stopped Coming Back To

![Sonic and Jet racing on Extreme Gear](/media/games/sonic-riders/sonic-riders-banner-main.jpg)

There are racing games you remember because they were technically perfect.

Then there are racing games you remember because they had a feeling.

*Sonic Riders* is one of the latter for me. I played it growing up, came back to it years later, and somehow it still has the same pull. I can start a race intending to play for a little while and end up continuing long past the point where I meant to stop.

It is one of my favorite racing games, and probably my favorite Sonic spin-off. That sounds excessive until the first turn arrives, the board hits the air, and the whole track opens up.

## A Sonic racer with its own idea

At a glance, *Sonic Riders* looks like a straightforward mascot racing game. Sonic, Tails, Knuckles, and a group of rivals race through bright tracks on futuristic boards.

But the Extreme Gear is not just a visual replacement for a kart.

The board changes the logic of the race. You are managing air, momentum, drift, terrain, shortcuts, and the timing of your movement through the course. The track is not only something to follow. It is something to read.

![Jet the Hawk on Extreme Gear](/media/games/sonic-riders/sonic-riders-jet.webp)

That is what gives the game its identity. It does not simply ask, “How fast can Sonic run?” It asks what happens when Sonic’s speed is redirected through a machine with its own rules.

The answer is a racer that feels more committed than it initially appears. Under the character designs and exaggerated presentation, there is a real arcade system waiting to be learned.

## The joy of catching air

The best moments in *Sonic Riders* happen when the race stops feeling flat.

You launch off a ramp, catch a current, grind across a rail, or cut through a section of the course that only becomes visible when you understand the track’s shape. The movement has a sense of lift. You are not only driving toward the finish line; you are trying to keep the whole route flowing.

![Sonic wearing goggles on Extreme Gear](/media/games/sonic-riders/sonic-riders-sonic.webp)

This is why the game can feel awkward for a few races and then suddenly become addictive. The controls are asking you to stop thinking of the board like a kart. Once that shift happens, the track becomes a chain of opportunities rather than a road with obstacles placed on it.

There is a satisfying rhythm to it: build speed, spend air, take the corner, recover, and prepare for the next section. A good race feels less like steering and more like maintaining a line through controlled chaos.

## Jet gave the game a rival with weight

Jet the Hawk is one of the reasons *Sonic Riders* works so well as a Sonic story.

He is not just another opponent selected from the character roster. He is a rival built around the same central fantasy as Sonic: speed, confidence, style, and the belief that he belongs at the front of the race.

![Jet the Hawk character artwork](/media/games/sonic-riders/sonic-riders-jet.webp)

Sonic is usually the obvious fastest character in the room. Jet complicates that assumption. He has the attitude of someone who thinks Sonic’s reputation is a problem to be corrected. The rivalry gives the game a little more friction than a simple hero-versus-villain setup.

The Babylon Rogues also bring a different visual language into the series. They look like they belong to a racing subculture: goggles, gear, boards, crews, and a sense that the world of *Sonic Riders* is bigger than the regular cast’s usual adventures.

That matters. The game feels like a side street in the Sonic universe rather than a remix of the main road.

## More technical than people expect

One reason *Sonic Riders* is underrated is that it hides its depth behind a bright presentation.

The characters are expressive. The courses are colorful. The story has the heightened tone of a Saturday-morning adventure. Nothing about the package announces that the player is about to spend time learning air management and route optimization.

![Sonic Riders PlayStation 2 cover](/media/games/sonic-riders/sonic-riders-jet-ps2-cover.jpg)

But the game rewards attention. Different characters and gear types alter how a race should be approached. A course that feels impossible with one setup can become manageable with another. The player gradually starts seeing which parts of a track are meant to be jumped, which are meant to be drifted, and which should be treated as a resource problem.

That process gives the game a satisfying middle ground. It is accessible enough to enjoy immediately, but specific enough to reward returning players.

## Why it still feels like 2006 in the best way

There is a particular kind of mid-2000s game energy in *Sonic Riders*.

It is colorful without being embarrassed by color. It has a strong logo, a loud soundtrack, rival characters with oversized personalities, and a story that treats futuristic racing as if it were the most important thing in the world.

The game is not trying to be quiet or prestige-oriented. It wants the boards to look cool. It wants the characters to pose. It wants the final lap to feel like a climax.

That directness is part of what I enjoy when I return to it. There is no need to translate the experience into something more respectable. *Sonic Riders* is a stylish arcade racer about Sonic characters flying through impossible courses, and it commits to that idea completely.

## Playing it now

I still play *Sonic Riders* through PCSX2 on Windows, which has made it easy to keep the game in rotation.

The hardware may have changed, but the game’s appeal has not. The image is cleaner, the setup is more convenient, and the old disc-era atmosphere is still there underneath it all. The menus, the character select screen, the course introductions, and the exaggerated rival banter bring back the feeling of a game that was meant to be played repeatedly rather than merely completed once.

Emulation also makes the game feel like part of an archive. It is not always easy to explain why this particular racing game matters to me. It does not have the broad cultural presence of *Mario Kart*, and it is not usually the first Sonic spin-off people mention.

But it has stayed playable in my memory because the system is memorable.

## The underrated favorite

The best racing games make you want to improve without making improvement feel like homework.

That is what *Sonic Riders* still does. It gives me a track worth learning, a machine worth understanding, and a cast that makes every race feel like part of a larger rivalry.

It is fast, strange, occasionally unforgiving, and much more technical than its cheerful presentation suggests. Most importantly, it has a personality of its own.

I do not return to *Sonic Riders* only because I played it growing up. I return because it still feels good to play. The board catches air, Jet appears in the next lane, the track bends upward, and for a moment the game becomes exactly what I remembered.

Not just a Sonic racing game.

My Sonic racing game.
