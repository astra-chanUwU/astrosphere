# The Starry Night

Vincent van Gogh painted *The Starry Night* in June 1889, looking from the asylum at Saint-Rémy toward the landscape beyond. The view begins with real things—a village, hills, a cypress, the moon, and stars—but the sky refuses to remain still. It rolls, curls, and turns the night into something almost physical.

The painting’s force comes from the tension between the quiet village below and the enormous movement above it. The dark cypress rises like a flame, while the stars and moon burn in yellow against layers of blue. Every brushstroke seems to carry direction: across the sky, around the stars, through the hills, and into the small windows glowing below.

It is tempting to treat the painting as a key to van Gogh’s inner life, but it can also simply be met as an image—one that makes looking feel active. Follow the currents of paint. Notice where the scene feels peaceful and where it begins to vibrate. Is the sky comforting, overwhelming, or both? Stay with it long enough for the answer to change.
