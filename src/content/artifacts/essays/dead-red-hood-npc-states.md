# Dead Red Hood: H Scenes and NPC States

> **Adult game / NSFW warning:** This entry discusses optional erotic scenes, sexual violence, coercion, and the ways NPCs can become frightened, injured, unconscious, or dead. The material is presented as documentation of the game's systems, not as erotic framing. Spoilers ahead.

*DEAD RED HOOD* gives the player control over whether its H scenes appear at all. Like the earlier *BLACKSOULS* games, it includes a setting that can turn those scenes off. The game's reported design also allows a complete clear while Red Hood remains a virgin.

That matters because the adult content is not described as a mandatory route through the game. Seeing an H scene is a player choice, and the systems around it include ways to avoid or interrupt some unwanted outcomes.

## The Rape Cancel System

The most unusual defensive mechanic is called the “Rape Cancel System.” When Red Hood is about to be raped, the player can press a button to summon Poro, who rescues her from the situation.

![Poro rushing to save Red Hood](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/poro-saves-red-hood.jpg)

The intended use is for an unintentional choice or an encounter that goes in a direction the player did not want. It is not a replacement for consent or a way to make the scene harmless; it is an emergency escape from a piece of sexual violence in an adult horror game.

Together with the H-scene toggle and the virgin-clear possibility, the system gives players several ways to set boundaries around the game's most sensitive material. A player can choose to see the scenes, disable them, or avoid sexual outcomes entirely while still completing the game.

## NPC states

NPCs can occupy one of ten reported states. These states track how a character relates to Red Hood, how vulnerable they are, and whether they can still participate in the mission.

### Normal

![Normal NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/normal.jpg)

**Normal** is the neutral starting point: the NPC is neither trusting Red Hood nor actively reacting to her as a threat.

### Trust

![Trust NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/trust.jpg)

**Trust** means the NPC trusts Red Hood. This may make conversation or manipulation easier, depending on the situation.

### Distrust

![Distrust NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/distrust.jpg)

**Distrust** means the NPC no longer accepts Red Hood at face value. The state sits between neutrality and open aggression, but it can make future interactions more difficult.

### Hostile

![Hostile NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/hostile.jpg)

**Hostile** means the NPC is actively against Red Hood. The reported options at this point are to fight back or escape, which makes the state a direct threat to the stealth mission.

### Lust

![Lust NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/lust.jpg)

**Lust** means the NPC desires Red Hood. In the game's darker routes, cruel NPCs may try to rape her in this state. This is one of the reasons the H-scene warning needs to be visible before discussing the system.

### Fear

![Fear NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/fear.jpg)

**Fear** means the NPC is scared of Red Hood. The state can occur after threatening someone or speaking to them while covered in blood, turning the aftermath of one encounter into a modifier for the next.

### Dying

![Dying NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/dying.jpg)

**Dying** means the NPC has been mortally wounded and is close to death. It is the clearest sign that an interaction has already crossed from intimidation or control into physical harm.

### Unconscious

![Unconscious NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/unconscious.jpg)

**Unconscious** means the NPC has been knocked out rather than killed. This can leave the player with a temporary opening, but it may also create a later problem if the NPC wakes or is discovered.

### Sleep

![Sleep NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/sleep.jpg)

**Sleep** is a non-combat vulnerable state. A sleeping NPC can be approached without the immediate reaction that would follow a normal conversation or a hostile encounter.

### Nihility

![Nihility NPC state](/media/games/dead-red-hood/h-scenes/nihility.jpg)

**Nihility** is the unexplained state. The translated description is given as “よぐっ,” identified in the available notes with Yog, or Yog-Sothoth. Because Yog appears in *BLACKSOULS II* as Mabel, the state may connect *DEAD RED HOOD* to that mythology—but this remains speculation until the developer explains it or a reliable translation becomes available.

The uncertainty is fitting. Most states describe a recognizable human reaction: trust, suspicion, fear, desire, injury, sleep. Nihility sounds like a condition in which the person has stopped being legible as an ordinary NPC at all.

## A system with boundaries

The H-scene settings and NPC states show two sides of *DEAD RED HOOD*. On one side, the game gives Red Hood a large and deliberately disturbing range of interactions, including sexual violence. On the other, it includes a way to disable H scenes, a rescue button for an unwanted rape route, and a complete-clear path that does not require Red Hood to have sex.

Those options do not erase the game's subject matter. They do make the player's control over exposure part of the design. For anyone approaching the game, the practical summary is: H scenes are optional, a virgin clear is possible, and the Rape Cancel System exists as an emergency interruption when a sexual-violence route begins.

The NPC states add another layer to the stealth system. Red Hood is not only moving through rooms and choosing assassination methods; she is constantly changing how the people around her perceive and withstand her. A gift can produce trust, a threat can produce fear, blood can alter the next conversation, and a failed interaction can turn a quiet mission hostile.

That makes the state system more important than a set of decorative labels. It is the social and physical memory of each mission—and one more way for the Black Trial's work to leave a mark on the world around it.
