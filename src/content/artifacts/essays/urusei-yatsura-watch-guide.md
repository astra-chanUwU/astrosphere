# Urusei Yatsura: Where to Begin with Lum and Ataru

Some romantic comedies are built around the moment when two people finally admit what they feel.

*Urusei Yatsura* is built around the much funnier problem of two people who are obviously attached to each other but would rather cause a public disaster than speak plainly.

Rumiko Takahashi’s manga began in *Weekly Shōnen Sunday* in 1978 and ran through 1987, eventually collected in 34 volumes. The premise is wonderfully simple: Ataru Moroboshi, a spectacularly unlucky and spectacularly flirtatious high-school student, becomes entangled with Lum, an electric alien oni girl who takes his accidental declaration of love seriously.

From there, the series expands into aliens, ghosts, gods, inventors, school friends, rivalries, misunderstandings, and an entire universe of people who make Ataru’s life more complicated while also making it much more interesting.

It is my favorite romcom because it never treats romance as a tidy road toward emotional maturity. Love is embarrassing, competitive, selfish, supernatural, hilarious, and occasionally very sweet.

## Start with the 2022 anime

For a first-time viewer, I recommend starting with the 2022 adaptation by David Production.

![Modern Lum and Ataru](/media/anime/urusei-yatsura/urusei-yatsura-lum-ataru.webp)

It is the cleanest introduction. The animation is bright and energetic, the character designs are easy to read, and the series moves through selected stories from the manga without asking a new viewer to navigate a long television history first. It aired across four cours for 46 episodes, giving the adaptation enough room to introduce the central cast while keeping the journey manageable.

The new series is also a good way to understand why the premise works. Ataru and Lum are not a conventional couple, but their chaos has a rhythm. He runs away, she chases him, somebody else makes the situation worse, and the episode finds a new way to turn romantic tension into a physical comedy routine.

The remake feels modern without sanding away the oddness. It is colorful, fast, and welcoming, but it still lets the characters be petty and ridiculous.

## Then go back to the original

After the 2022 series, the original television anime becomes even more rewarding.

![Classic Lum among the planets](/media/anime/urusei-yatsura/urusei-yatsura-space-lum-classic.webp)

The classic series has a looser, more lived-in feeling. It comes from a different television culture: more room for detours, more standalone episodes, more visual experiments, and a willingness to let a joke continue until it has become completely unreasonable.

The older animation also gives the world a particular warmth. The colors, music, backgrounds, and character acting carry the atmosphere of a beloved late-night rerun. Even when the plot is absurd, the series can feel strangely domestic: a classroom, a shopping street, a bedroom, a festival, and then suddenly a haunted planet or a godlike visitor has entered the afternoon.

![Classic Lum in an 80s cityscape](/media/anime/urusei-yatsura/urusei-yatsura-lum-aesthetic-80s.webp)

The classic version is not simply a longer version of the remake. It has its own pacing and personality. The 2022 show is the better doorway; the original is the larger house.

## The opening song is part of the spell

The opening theme, **ラムのラブソング** — “Lum’s Love Song” — is one of those songs that seems to define an entire anime before the first scene has properly begun.

The player above is a local archive copy. For normal listening, use an official or licensed release where available.

The song captures the series perfectly: bright, mischievous, romantic, and just a little shameless. It does not sound like a solemn declaration. It sounds like someone has fallen in love and intends to make that everybody else’s problem.

## A beginner-friendly movie order

The classic films are best watched in release order. They do not all function as strict steps in one continuous plot, and some are more interested in atmosphere or formal experimentation than continuity.

1. **Urusei Yatsura: Only You** — a playful, large-scale romantic adventure.
2. **Urusei Yatsura 2: Beautiful Dreamer** — the most famous and most dreamlike film, directed by Mamoru Oshii.
3. **Urusei Yatsura 3: Remember My Love** — a more fairy-tale-shaped story about memory, transformation, and attachment.
4. **Urusei Yatsura 4: Lum the Forever** — an atmospheric film concerned with Lum’s place in the world.
5. **Urusei Yatsura 5: The Final Chapter** — save this for the end of the classic television-and-film journey; it adapts the manga’s concluding story.
6. **Urusei Yatsura: Always My Darling** — the final theatrical film, best treated as a later celebratory side story rather than a replacement for the emotional ending of *The Final Chapter*.

![The classic and modern Lum designs](/media/anime/urusei-yatsura/urusei-yatsura-lum-diff-variant.webp)

If you only have time for two films, watch *Only You* and *Beautiful Dreamer*. The first gives you a strong sense of the franchise’s romantic-comedy machinery. The second shows how strange, reflective, and visually ambitious *Urusei Yatsura* can become.

## Why the old series still feels timeless

The classic *Urusei Yatsura* is old, but it does not feel trapped in its period.

Part of that comes from the premise. Alien visitors, impossible technology, ghosts, folklore, and school gossip can all exist in the same story without needing to be reconciled into one tidy genre. The series is comfortable being science fiction in one scene and a neighborhood farce in the next.

![Classic Lum portrait](/media/anime/urusei-yatsura/urusei-yatsura-lum-classic-pfp.webp)

The other part is the comedy. Ataru is not a conventional romantic lead, and Lum is not a passive ideal. They collide. They compete. They hurt each other’s pride. Their relationship is funny because it is unstable, but it keeps becoming more emotionally real despite both of them trying to avoid an honest conversation.

That contradiction still works because it is human underneath the aliens and electricity.

## The best way to meet the series

My recommendation is simple:

Start with the 2022 series. Let it introduce Lum, Ataru, and the madhouse around them. Then move to the classic anime when you want more episodes, stranger detours, and the full old-school atmosphere. Watch the films in release order, but keep *The Final Chapter* for the end of the classic story.

![Lum and Ataru in the modern series](/media/anime/urusei-yatsura/urusei-yatsura-lum-ataru-jelly.webp)

The new series may be the easiest way in, but knowing it makes the original even better. You begin to notice how much personality lives in the older timing, the looser animation, the music, and the long pauses before somebody makes an unforgivable decision.

And then there is Lum: floating through every version of the series with her tiger stripes, green hair, electric temper, and impossible confidence.

She is the spark that keeps the whole thing alive.

![Lum and the cast](/media/anime/urusei-yatsura/urusei-yatsura-thank-you.webp)

*Urusei Yatsura* is not only a classic romcom. It is a whole atmosphere: neon skies, school uniforms, cosmic interruptions, old theme songs, and two people who cannot stop chasing each other long enough to admit that they already belong together.
