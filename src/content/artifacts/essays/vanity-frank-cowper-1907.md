# Vanity

Frank Cadogan Cowper’s *Vanity* (1907) presents beauty as something lavish, deliberate, and slightly theatrical. A woman in ornate clothing holds a small mirror while pearls, grapes, patterned fabric, and dark leaves gather around her. The painting belongs to the late Pre-Raphaelite taste for intense colour, historical costume, and carefully composed symbolism.

The mirror explains the title, but it does not settle the mood. Her half-closed eyes can suggest self-admiration, boredom, or the calm of someone accustomed to being watched. The grapes and jewellery promise abundance, while the older vanitas tradition quietly reminds us that beauty and pleasure do not last.

Look at the face, the mirror, and the heavy gold-and-red surface around them. Is she absorbed in her reflection, performing vanity for us, or quietly judging the whole display? The painting offers a warning, but it also clearly enjoys everything it claims is temporary. Let that tension stay unresolved.
