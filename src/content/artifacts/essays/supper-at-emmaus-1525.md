# Supper at Emmaus — Pontormo

In Jacopo Pontormo’s *Supper at Emmaus* (1525), recognition happens quietly. After the Resurrection, Christ meets two disciples on the road to Emmaus. They do not know him until the meal, when he takes the bread, blesses it, and passes it around the table.

Pontormo makes this moment feel both intimate and uncanny. Christ sits at the centre in deep blue, one hand raised and the other resting near the bread. The disciples lean toward him, absorbed in the meal, while monks and other figures gather close behind. Above them, an eye inside a triangle opens in the darkness, turning the simple room into something watched from beyond itself.

Look at the hands, the bread, the crowded edges, and the way the black background makes every face and colour appear suddenly present. Is this a scene of revelation, ordinary hospitality, or a moment before understanding arrives? Let the painting’s quiet strangeness remain with you before settling on an answer.
