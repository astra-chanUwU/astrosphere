# Maid Victoria

Maid Victoria is one of *BLACKSOULS I*’s first companions: a quiet attendant from the House of Truth whose route turns service, shame, and self-destruction into a grim fairy-tale portrait. Her story continues into *BLACKSOULS II*, where the maid’s private bargain becomes a larger covenant with violence.

> **Adults-only warning:** Victoria’s route includes suicide, sexual coercion, rape branches, nudity, demonbeast violence, and other disturbing material. The H-scene section identifies the source guide’s nine scenes without reproducing explicit prose.

## Appearances in BLACKSOULS I

### Meeting Victoria

Victoria waits in the House of Truth, speaking with the formal politeness expected of a maid while quietly revealing how little control she has over her own life. The player can question her, choose whether to embrace her, and eventually invite her into the companion roster.

Her route is built on small acts of care: dialogue, reassurance, and the promise that someone will return. That gentleness is set against a house whose “truth” is inseparable from punishment.

### Victoria’s suicide and Hotel Poseidon

Victoria’s despair eventually becomes literal. If the route is mishandled, she kills herself, making the player’s earlier choices feel less like flavor and more like responsibility. Hotel Poseidon offers an adult branch that places intimacy beside that crisis rather than resolving it.

## Miscellaneous route branches

### The Basement

The Basement branch turns Victoria’s maid identity into a structure of confinement. The scene is explicitly sexual and coercive; it should be read as horror-route documentation, not as a romantic reward.

### Endings A, C, and the Companion Ending

Victoria’s endings divide between rescue, loss, and uneasy companionship. Ending A and the companion ending preserve different versions of her devotion, while Ending C makes the costs of leaving her inside Elysium’s machinery impossible to ignore.

### Covenant, Leaf’s Ring, sisters, and Lovecraft Cinema

Victoria’s covenant is the sequel’s clearest expansion of her story. She bargains for power, reacts to Leaf’s Ring, and is placed beside her sisters in a sequence that shows how individual suffering becomes family history. Lovecraft Cinema gives the route one more stage on which identity and spectacle can collide.

## Personality

Victoria is reserved, dutiful, anxious, and deeply hungry for approval. She uses service as a moral language because it is the only language her household has taught her. Her politeness is not emptiness; it is a survival skill.

The route’s tragedy is that Victoria often mistakes being useful for being safe. When the player treats her as a person, she can become a companion. When he treats her as an object, the game’s violence confirms the fear she already carries.

## Design

Victoria’s maid uniform, pale hair, and restrained expression contrast with the increasingly monstrous images attached to her route. The design does not abandon the maid silhouette when the story becomes violent; it makes that silhouette carry the violence.
