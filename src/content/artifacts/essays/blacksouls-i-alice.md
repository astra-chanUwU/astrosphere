# Alice

Alice is an important character across both BLACKSOULS games. She is based on Lewis Carroll’s Alice from *Alice in Wonderland*, but the series multiplies her into a set of roles, memories, masks, and entities. This page follows the source guide’s appearances in *BLACKSOULS I*, *BLACKSOULS II*, and DLC3’s Winterbell sequence.

> **Spoiler warning:** This page covers endings A–H, Alice Liddell’s identity, the Crawling One, and disturbing adult-game material. Adult scenes are acknowledged but not described explicitly.

## Appearances | BLACKSOULS I

In the first game, Alice appears immediately and then returns in brief, easy-to-miss appearances.

### Meeting Alice

Alice congratulates the player for being chosen as the protagonist, introduces herself, and asks for a name. After the class choice, she gives Grimm a present for the beginning of the journey and asks whether adult scenes should be enabled. She announces the start of the story, then dies and becomes a pool of blood.

### Ending D

After Baphomet is defeated, Leaf is left alone with Grimm in a black space. She imagines that the two of them can become lovers and create the most beautiful story together. Alice suddenly appears, claps at the scene, and tells Leaf that it was amusing. Alice is delighted to see Grimm again, and the game ends with Ending D.

### Riverside Sighting

After Ending D, Alice can be seen in the upper-right corner of the Riverside Bank area in Wonderland. The small appearance becomes a recurring landmark in the sequel.

## Appearances | BLACKSOULS II

Alice appears far more often in the second game. The sequel begins by asking the player to choose which Alice will accompany Grimm through the prologue.

### The Three Alices

The three starting choices are Mother Alice, Little Sister Alice, and Daughter Alice. Older Sister Alice existed before disappearing from Wonderland. The selected Alice disappears during the prologue, but can be saved in several endings. The choice can later be changed in Queensland, and each Alice has distinct dialogue, interactions, adult-route variations, and a unique nightmare form.

### Prologue

If the prologue is not skipped, someone calls Grimm’s name and he chooses which Alice is beside him. Each version has a different personality and opening scene.

#### Mother Alice

Mother Alice is older, motherly, and dominant. Grimm wakes with his head on her lap beneath a tree. She brushes his hair, calls him her dear boy, remembers how long it has been since she visited the riverside, and asks whether they should always live together. She talks about her clothes and the difficulty of finding someone who appreciates Gothic Lolita fashion before Grimm falls asleep and wakes to find her gone.

#### Little Sister Alice

Little Sister Alice is energetic and caring. Grimm wakes while she feeds him during a picnic and repeatedly calls him “Big Brother.” She is proud of the food she cooked, promises to make another lunch, and hugs him because she does not want the holiday to end. The screen begins flashing; she panics and begs Grimm not to die before disappearing.

#### Daughter Alice

Daughter Alice is sweet and innocent. Sitting in Grimm’s lap with a picture book, she calls him “Papa” and asks him to finish the story of a knight, dragon, and princess. She imagines Grimm as a knight who will rescue her and take her to a castle, then asks him to take her to an amusement park. She disappears when Grimm begins to fall asleep again.

## Early encounters

### Spore Forest Encounter

At the beginning of the game, before Grimm has earned a Progression Point, a high-SEN route can reveal one of the three Alices at the Spore Forest bonfire. Interacting with her starts an adult scene in the game and makes her disappear afterwards. The encounter grants a Girl’s Scent and one Progression Point. Carrying a Candy changes Daughter Alice’s reaction.

### Riverside Sighting

When Grimm first enters Riverside, he sees a figure in front of the giant tree through the fog. The figure is Jabberwock, not Alice. Afterwards, at 0 SEN, an Alice sighting can trigger in the upper-right section of the area. Alice disappears, but red advice marks combined with Prickett’s Ring reveal a message telling Grimm not to cheat on her. The position references her Riverside Bank appearance from the first game.

## Alice and the endings

### Endings A, C, D, and E

At 10 Progression Points, Node waits at the upper door of Library Dream and leads Grimm toward what appears to be Alice’s room. Cheshire Cat is inside and explains that she died not long after giving birth, so the Alice she remembers is only a little girl. She remembers Alice as sweet and innocent, and recalls being petted as a treasured cat.

Beyond the door, Grimm fights the Tearing Beast Dinah with Alice behind her. Failing to defeat Dinah kills both Grimm and Alice for Ending C. After the fight, Grimm reunites with the Alice selected at the beginning of the cycle. The apparently happy choice leads to Ending A, while killing Alice at high SEN leads to Ending D. At low SEN, killing her reveals her true form and starts a boss fight; victory leads to Ending E.

The three nightmare identities are different: Mother Alice becomes the Elk-Goddess, Little Sister Alice becomes the Shadow Demoness, and Daughter Alice becomes the Black One.

### Endings F and G

After fulfilling the route conditions, Grimm must reject the three Alices at the beginning of a new cycle. A fourth option, Alice Liddell, appears. Choosing it leads to the Little Girl’s Nightmare and an Alice with a black face. The guide identifies this figure as the Crawling One, an entity from the Cthulhu Mythos also known as Nyarlathotep.

Siding with Mary Sue leads to Ending F. Mary explains that the Crawling One deceived both her and Grimm, and Grimm eventually faces Little Girl Alice in the Love area. Siding with Red Hood leads to Ending G. Alice Liddell offers Grimm a paradise Wonderland, but the encounter becomes another prison; Red Hood and Poro intervene, the Crawling Little Girls appear, and the garden collapses.

The ending separates Alice Liddell from the entity connected to her. Alice cries for Lewis Carroll, her teacher, while another presence leaves Grimm’s body and leads her away into the white world.

### Winterbell Visual Novel

In Winterbell, Alice walks ahead of Grimm without noticing him. Following her leads to an estate and a visual-novel sequence with Alice Liddell, Lorina Liddell, and Edith Liddell. The opening conversation concerns Alice’s inactivity in class and Lewis Carroll’s defense of her studies; the guide identifies Mr. Robinson as probably Robinson Duckworth, Carroll’s real-life friend.

The estate first looks innocent, then becomes increasingly hostile. Alice tells Grimm about a dead girl who sometimes appears to the sisters and hates adults. Alice scares Grimm in the living room, scolds Dinah after a plate breaks, and invites him to visit her room during the night. On the third night, she calls for help because the unnamed girl is present. Alice says she cannot remember her own name, and her face begins to crack.

The text becomes unreadable unless Grimm has Prickett’s Ring. Alice says that she has found him, that he must search for Alice, and that he must never forget or stop loving Alice. When the night ends, her body is found dead. Edith’s face is later overtaken by black substance, Lorina runs away, and strange figures haunt the house. In the living room, Mytyl, another figure, and Mary Sue discuss Carroll and what to do with his soul. Lorina eventually kills herself in the vestibule; Grimm can leave by continuing through the door, using the key found on Alice’s corpse if necessary.

#### Dead Alice

If Grimm ignores Alice’s request on the third night and lets the night pass, he wakes to a manic breakdown. Alice’s mutilated corpse appears in his room, nailed to the ceiling, with bloody writing covering the wall.

#### Hide and Seek

Grimm can also avoid the Liddell sisters entirely after they welcome him into the house. If he avoids them and lets the days pass, he can leave through the vestibule on the third day. The exit asks whether he wants to turn around; turning back lets Alice find him and prevents his escape.

### Ending H

Ending H, which stands for Hell, presents too many Alices to count.

#### Little Sister Alice Intervenes

After Grand Guignol and Grimm’s farewell to Node, Daughter Alice appears first. Grimm runs and encounters Mother Alice, then Little Sister Alice blocks the exit. Little Sister Alice attacks the other two and tells Grimm to stand still. If Grimm tries to save her because he loves his little sister, she becomes furious and asks who his little sister is, pointing toward the more important girl he is still searching for.

The scene reveals the other Alices’ identities through the entities above and behind them: Jubjub appears above Little Sister Alice, Bandersnatch behind Daughter Alice, and Jabberwock behind Mother Alice.

#### The Crawling One’s Last Appearance

When Red Hood tries to save Grimm from the black void, faceless Alices hold him down and pull him into the abyss. Grimm wakes surrounded by countless Alices, unsure of his own identity. A strange Alice embraces him and asks whether this is what he truly wanted. The Crawling One apologizes, takes Grimm’s hand, and leads him into the white world, where she lets go and tells him to forget her.

Grimm promises that he will never forget Alice and that they will reunite as lovers. After the credits, the game displays **Heartbroken END**.

## All Alices

The source guide names Alice-02, Alice Liddell, Alice as the Crawling One, Mother Alice, Little Sister Alice, Daughter Alice, Doll Alice, and Red Idol Prickett’s Alice-shaped appearances. The point of the list is not to settle which one is authentic. It shows how Alice’s face functions as a mask, a memory, a role, and a doorway into the game’s deeper mythology.

## Personality

Alice is loving, caring, high-spirited, and decisive. She knows what she wants, and her warmth makes the later merciless or unreadable versions feel like violations of a person rather than unrelated monsters. The three sequel versions express that same attachment through different roles: Mother Alice offers possession through care, Little Sister Alice offers closeness through energetic dependence, and Daughter Alice offers safety through storybook innocence.

## Design

The ordinary Alice has long blonde hair, blue eyes, a white-and-blue apron dress, and a black ribbon. Her design uses one of the most recognizable visual languages associated with *Alice in Wonderland*, so the player can identify her even when the story refuses to identify what she is.

### The Three Alices

The three Alices retain the same silhouette while changing the face and body language. Mother Alice has a larger face, narrow eyes, a head ribbon, and a taller, more developed frame. Little Sister Alice has a sharper face, tender eyes, bunny ears, and a Black Rabbit pin; her physique is closest to the original Alice. Daughter Alice has large cutesy eyes, a rounder face, shorter hair, a hair bow, and a much smaller frame.
