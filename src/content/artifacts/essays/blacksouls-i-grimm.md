# Grimm

Grimm is the player character, but *BLACKSOULS* treats that apparent simplicity as a veil. The guide identifies him as an amalgamation of fairy-tale authors—explicitly including Lewis Carroll, the Brothers Grimm, and Hans Christian Andersen—whose combined identity is Mary Sue’s work rather than a natural biography.

## A protagonist made of authors

That construction explains why the series can make so many incompatible stories feel personal. Grimm moves through fairy-tale worlds as a participant and an authorial residue. He can be heroic, frightened, cruel, or confused because the game has made him a crowded vessel rather than a clean fantasy avatar.

## Hotel Poseidon and the fracture

The Hotel Poseidon flashbacks make the premise emotionally legible. Souls inside Grimm argue, panic, and fail to agree on a single self. Instead of using the author references as trivia, the sequence makes them a source of pressure: a person who contains several literary inheritances cannot easily decide what his own desire means.

The late-game revelations should therefore be read as a warning about authorship as much as identity. Grimm is Mary’s “magnum opus,” but he is still a person trying to choose a path through a world built from somebody else’s stories.
