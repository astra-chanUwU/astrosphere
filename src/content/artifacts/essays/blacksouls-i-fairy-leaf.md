# Fairy Leaf

Fairy Leaf is the first companion in *BLACKSOULS I*: a green fairy in the upper Holy Forest who becomes the player’s guide, bonfire attendant, and one of the game’s most important hidden authors. She looks like a small, cheerful helper. The longer the route continues, the less stable that first impression becomes.

Leaf is also one of the Great Ones. In the first game she is Mary Sue, the being who controls Elysium and built its stories around Grimm. In the sequel she becomes Mary Ann, a larger, imprisoned version of the same character whose power to change the world has been taken away. Her route is therefore both companion story and cosmology: it starts with advice at a bonfire and ends by asking who is allowed to author a world.

## Appearances in BLACKSOULS I

Leaf remains beside the player throughout the first game. She is the first companion and can be summoned in battle, but her presence is not only mechanical. Her short lines at bonfires quietly identify the area, warn about danger, and teach the player what kind of freedom this world offers.

### Meeting Leaf

Leaf waits in the upper Holy Forest with her back turned, singing as if the forest were an ordinary fairy-tale clearing. The first choice is telling: greet her or try to kill her. A greeting makes her the player’s guide. She introduces herself, asks about the Lost Empire, and offers to become the first companion.

Her advice is deliberately alarming. You are “free,” she says: kill people you dislike, take what you want, and remember that the country is destined to perish anyway. She says it with the brightness of a fairy offering directions, which is exactly why the scene feels wrong.

### Basement, Empty Beach, and the Gate of the Lost Empire

Leaf can also be found in the Basement, where her teasing line makes the prison’s purpose unmistakable. At the Empty Beach, before Atlantica, she wanders briefly and disappears after the player reaches the city.

After four Demonic Princesses are dead, she appears at the Gate of the Lost Empire. There she explains the history behind Cinderella’s catastrophe: a dreaming ash-haired girl was given too much magic, the pumpkin carriage attacked, the glass shoes shattered, and the fog spread through Elysium. Leaf’s mother vanished to atone for her sins.

Leaf offers to face Cinderella with the player, then retreats into a more honest confession: she wants to be with you more and more, and running away is allowed. If you continue, she gives you Leaf’s Ring. The ring grants attack and spell evasion plus 20 percent and doubles speed; it is also important to later routes in *BLACKSOULS II*.

### Hotel Poseidon

At level 100 or higher, Leaf can be taken to Hotel Poseidon through Pinocchio. The source guide describes the scene as deliberately morbid because of Leaf’s size, and notes that killing her is not available there. This belongs behind the adults-only warning: the route combines sexual intimacy with an intentionally unsettling scale contrast.

## Miscellaneous interactions in BLACKSOULS I

### Fighting Leaf

The first meeting can become a fight instead of a greeting. Leaf is overwhelmingly strong at the start, but a sufficiently powerful player can defeat her and receive a Black Soul, Fairy Powder, and Fairy’s Wing. Even after losing, Leaf treats the encounter as part of the tale’s progress and returns to her place.

### Hansel and Gretel

After Lindamea deals with Hansel and Gretel, Leaf stands outside Dorothy’s house. Her reflection on humans killing and dying without blinking is one of the route’s clearest examples of her morbid curiosity. She does not excuse the children or rewrite what happened. She simply observes that the story ended there.

### Playing in the Water

At level 30 or higher, Leaf can be found in the southern Holy Forest, playing in the lake. The scene is flirtatious and adults-only, but its tone is closer to teasing than revelation: Leaf invites attention, mocks the player’s embarrassment, and then laughs when the player leaves.

### Ending A

Leaf appears near the Prayer Master at the lamppost. She celebrates the death of the Demonic Princesses, calls the player a hero, thanks them for saving everyone, and says that she loves them. A second conversation lets the player begin the celebration or delay it.

### Ending C and Mary Sue

The C Ending is where Leaf’s surface story breaks open. After the Alice’s Library is filled with every fairy tale and the lever in the Kill area is pulled, the player reaches an original, chaotic version of the Holy Forest. The surrounding conversations point toward Mary Sue.

Mary Sue is Leaf, a Great One who controls Elysium and created the garden for Grimm. She is not simply a hidden villain; she is an obsessive author who made a world so the player could stay with her inside it. When the player reaches the End of the Journey, Leaf asks for Mary Sue’s identity. The correct answer is “Leaf.” A wrong answer leads to a game over.

### Fighting Mary Sue

Once Leaf’s identity is revealed, Mary Sue initiates a sequence of increasingly powerful forms. The first battle is followed by Demonbeast Mary Sue and then God of Creation Mary Sue. The route turns a familiar companion into the final authorial obstacle: someone who can write the rules, rewrite bodies, and still be defeated inside the story she made.

After the final battle, the scene becomes sexual and violent. This is an adults-only route with a rape-and-death branch, not a conventional romantic reward. The source guide counts it as one of Leaf’s H scenes and records it alongside the other route events.

## Appearances in BLACKSOULS II

### Imprisoned Fairy and Mary Ann

In *BLACKSOULS II*, Leaf is imprisoned in the Fairy Jail inside Queen’s Castle in Queensland, Prickett’s garden. Her name becomes Mary Ann, a likely reference to the White Rabbit’s housemaid in *Alice in Wonderland*. She is now the player’s size rather than a tiny fairy.

The jail scene allows the player to leave her, punish her, kill her, or save her. Saving her does not restore the old freedom: her ability to change things has been removed, and an unknown force prevents her from leaving. If she is saved, the player can later make love to her and receive Leaf’s Ring again.

### Endings F and G

During the F and G endings, Mary Ann meets the player and Red Hood after Node helps her escape. The player must choose whether to side with Red Hood or Mary Ann. The Red Hood path turns into a fight against Mary; the Mary Ann path turns the two women against Red Hood.

The route then moves toward Alice and the idea that Mary’s stories have trapped everyone inside a cycle of obsession, betrayal, and repetition. The source guide’s images preserve that progression: reunion, choice, violence, and the promise of an eternity that never arrives.

### Losing the Ability to Change

A teddy bear in Winterbell reveals a flashback about Mary losing her power. Node tells her that her creations have harmed other Great Ones and that she will never be a Great One again. Mary responds by calling herself a kidnapped princess waiting for a prince. The scene ends with an ominous visitor, most likely Jabberwock.

### Ending H

Mary makes a short appearance near the end of Ending H, passing through a dark void before disappearing.

## Personality

### Fairy Leaf

As Fairy Leaf, she is sweet, mischievous, attentive, and devoted to the player. She is always waiting at the next bonfire and is delighted when you feed her souls. Her sweetness is real, but it is also theatrical: she knows how much the player relies on her and turns guidance into intimacy.

### Mary Sue

As Mary Sue, the same devotion becomes possessive authorship. She made Elysium so she could remain with Grimm forever, even if that required turning every heroine into part of a tragic story. She loves the player and hurts the player; she is attached to him even when the relationship becomes violent and impossible.

## Design

Leaf’s design is immediately legible: short green hair, large green eyes, a tiny fairy body, and a green bodysuit with very little ornament. The sequel’s Mary Ann keeps the face and identity while changing the scale, which makes the transformation from companion to imprisoned Great One visible before the dialogue explains it.
