# Holy Knight Jeanne

Holy Knight Jeanne is the first knight the player meets in *BLACKSOULS I*: a Joan of Arc-inspired companion whose faith, courtesy, and courage survive in a world built from fog, demonbeasts, and cruel fairy-tale reversals. Her route begins as a rescue and quickly becomes a story about resurrection, consent, and what happens when a moral person is forced into Elysium’s systems.

> **Adults-only warning:** Jeanne’s route includes death, sexual coercion, rape branches, nudity, and violence. The H-scene section below identifies the source guide’s seven *BLACKSOULS I* scenes without reproducing explicit prose.

## Appearances in BLACKSOULS I

### Meeting Jeanne

The prologue opens with the player imprisoned at Fort Ivern. Jeanne breaks into the cell, recognizes a strange undead who still carries a spark of life, and urges him to move his feet and bring light into the world. She introduces herself as Jeanne d’Arc of Orleans and offers to escort him toward the fairy lands.

Her promise to protect the player is interrupted by Drake Helkaiser. The dragon burns Jeanne alive in front of him, making her death one of the game’s first lessons: heroism is real here, but it is not protected.

### Reviving Jeanne

Jeanne’s ashes remain at the prologue’s endpoint. Before the four Demonic Princesses are defeated, the player can carry them—and a Kraken Tentacle—to Shaman Keto in the Skeleton Maze. Keto’s ritual restores Jeanne imperfectly; she returns confused, starved of souls, and vulnerable to the player’s choice.

Feeding her souls saves her and unlocks the companion route. The alternate choice sends her to the Basement and frames her through sexual domination. Either way, the resurrection is intentionally unstable: Jeanne remembers terror, asks who the player is, and clings to the warmth of a living body.

### The Holy Forest

Once revived, Jeanne waits in the Holy Forest, the land of fairies. She explains that only people who have lost their destination can reach it and wonders whether that describes her. Her uncertainty contrasts with the certainty of the knight from the prologue; faith remains important, but it is no longer simple.

## Appearances in BLACKSOULS II

Jeanne does not receive a full companion route in the sequel. Her belongings and the Hotel Poseidon flashback keep her present as memory: an absent knight whose earlier kindness is recalled through objects, fragments, and a scene that reframes the first game’s intimacy.

## Personality

Jeanne is moral, polite, thankful, and unusually sincere for Elysium. Sin repulses her; if the player accumulates too much sin, she can refuse to remain his companion. That boundary matters because it gives her an ethical position inside a game that repeatedly treats characters as collectible routes.

Her bravery is not hardness. She is frightened, she grieves, and she needs reassurance after resurrection. The route’s emotional center is the tension between her ideal of protection and the player’s ability to use her.

## Design

BLACKSOULS’ Jeanne departs from the common dark-haired Joan of Arc image. She has long blonde hair, blue eyes, pale armor, and a soft expression that makes her read as gentle before the script asks her to fight. The final gallery image places her beside a fluffy beast and a llama, preserving the series’ habit of mixing sacred iconography with absurd fairy-tale comedy.
