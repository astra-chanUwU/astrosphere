# Black Bailiff Charlotte: Red Hood's Devoted Junior

> **Translation note:** *DEAD RED HOOD* is unreleased and does not yet have an official English translation. This character note is based on translated development material, so some details remain provisional.

Charlotte is one of the characters who makes the *DEAD RED HOOD* thread feel immediately more personal. The game is full of institutions, sinners, executions, and strange cities, but Black Bailiff Charlotte introduces a different kind of tension: a new member of the Black Trial who is intensely attached to Red Hood.

She is Red Hood's junior, a quiet newcomer to the organization, and she likes Red Hood a lot. The available description says that she dislikes Garm, but becomes very attached to Red Hood instead. The distance between them is described as unusually close—and the artwork makes that subtext wonderfully difficult to ignore.

![Black Bailiff Charlotte](/media/games/dead-red-hood/charlotte/black-bailiff-charlotte.jpg)

## A junior in the Black Trial

Charlotte is introduced as a new member of the Black Trial rather than as an outsider who wanders into Red Hood's mission. That gives the relationship a useful structure. Red Hood has seniority and experience; Charlotte is the junior who follows close behind, watches her, and seems to have decided that attachment is more important than professional distance.

The original Japanese description characterizes her as a gloomy newcomer and Red Hood's junior. It also says that she dislikes Garm while becoming very attached to Red Hood. The final phrase emphasizes how close that attachment is, but leaves the exact nature of the relationship open enough for the game's scenes to develop it.

That ambiguity is part of the appeal. Charlotte does not need a confession in the first image for the yuri energy to be obvious. She is already leaning into Red Hood's space, already finding reasons to remain near her, already looking like the Black Trial's most dangerous example of a workplace crush.

## Decapitation Scissors

Charlotte's execution tool is the Decapitation Scissors, or **断ち切り鋏** in the supplied Japanese description. It is an excellent weapon for her: domestic, oversized, intimate, and immediately threatening.

![Charlotte with her Decapitation Scissors](/media/games/dead-red-hood/charlotte/charlotte-with-scissors.jpg)

The scissors also connect her to the fairy-tale and storybook logic around the Black Trial. Charlotte looks small and withdrawn, but the weapon turns that quietness into something sharper. She is not merely Red Hood's shy junior. She is an executioner with a tool designed to close a distance permanently.

The contrast is especially good in a cast built around animal forms, cloaks, and symbolic punishments. Red Hood carries the visual weight of the fairy tale; Charlotte carries the quiet mechanism that cuts it apart.

## The yuri of it all

I love that the game's darker world makes room for this particular kind of tenderness. Red Hood and Charlotte are repeatedly shown cuddling, sharing warmth, and moving toward a kiss. The images do not feel like generic friendship illustrations. Charlotte's attachment has a romantic charge, and the relationship is one of the clearest sources of softness in a setting otherwise organized around fear and execution.

![Red Hood and Charlotte cuddling together](/media/games/dead-red-hood/charlotte/red-hood-and-charlotte-snuggled.jpg)

![Red Hood and Charlotte wrapped closely together](/media/games/dead-red-hood/charlotte/red-hood-and-charlotte-snuggled-2.jpg)

The best part is that Charlotte's affection does not make her visually harmless. Her design is all dark hair, black clothing, hidden expression, and enormous scissors. Then she leans against Red Hood or tries to kiss her, and the character becomes something else: not less dangerous, but more emotionally exposed.

![Charlotte wants to kiss Red Hood](/media/games/dead-red-hood/charlotte/charlotte-wants-to-kiss-red-hood.jpg)

That contrast gives the pairing its flavor. Red Hood is the person Charlotte trusts enough to approach. The same Charlotte who looks unreadable beneath her hood becomes visibly needy, shy, and close when Red Hood is near. It is a small emotional rebellion against the Black Trial's cold machinery.

## Charlotte and Garm

Charlotte's dislike of Garm adds friction to the relationship web. Garm belongs to the same executioner world, but Charlotte does not seem interested in sharing Red Hood's attention with him. The jealousy may be comic, hostile, or something more serious; with the game still unreleased, it is safer to describe the tension than to decide what it means.

That triangle also gives Charlotte a way to be more than a love interest. Her attachment to Red Hood changes how she relates to another member of the Black Trial, and her feelings can affect the atmosphere of the group. She is not simply placed beside Red Hood for decorative intimacy. She has loyalties, dislikes, and a weapon of her own.

![Charlotte looking cute and bloodied](/media/games/dead-red-hood/charlotte/charlotte-cute-and-bloody.jpg)

The bloodied portrait is a good reminder of that. Charlotte can be adorable, but “adorable” is not the same as safe in *DEAD RED HOOD*. The game's tenderness and violence sit very close together. A cute expression can share a frame with blood; a cuddle can exist inside an organization built around executions.

## Who is Charlotte based on?

The name may point in more than one direction. One possibility is Charlotte Grimm, described in the available notes as a sister of the Brothers Grimm. Another is Charlotte Corday, the assassin who killed Jean-Paul Marat during the French Revolution.

Both possibilities fit a character who combines storybook associations, intimate closeness, and assassination. But neither identification is confirmed. Charlotte may not be based on either historical figure, and the resemblance may be a deliberate mixture rather than a one-to-one reference.

For now, the strongest evidence is the character herself: a gloomy new bailiff, Red Hood's junior, Garm's apparent enemy, and a girl with a pair of decapitation scissors who gets very close to the woman she likes.

## A small bright thread in a dark game

The *BLACKSOULS* universe is interested in desire, guilt, punishment, and the way fairy tales become ugly when their moral machinery is exposed. Charlotte fits that world because her affection is not detached from its violence. She belongs to the Black Trial. She carries an execution tool. She is still capable of wanting warmth, closeness, and Red Hood's attention.

That is why this side content matters to me. It is not only a character profile; it is a little pocket of yuri inside a game about sinners and executioners. Charlotte's closeness to Red Hood makes the Black Trial feel less like a distant monster organization and more like a place where people can form messy, tender, jealous attachments.

I am very ready to see what the finished game does with them.
