# Supper at Emmaus — Caravaggio

Caravaggio’s *Supper at Emmaus* (1601) catches recognition at the exact moment it breaks open. At an ordinary dinner table, two disciples realize that the quiet traveller among them is the risen Christ. One throws his arms outward; the other pushes himself forward, as if the discovery has entered the room with physical force.

Caravaggio brings the miracle close enough to touch. The figures press against the edge of the table, their gestures reaching into the viewer’s space. The fruit, bread, wine, and worn clothing feel familiar and immediate, while the dark background removes almost everything that might distract from the faces and hands.

Look at the uneven light, the basket of fruit, and the way the disciples’ bodies expand beyond the frame. The scene is sacred, but it is also awkward, crowded, and wonderfully human. Does the miracle arrive through Christ’s identity, the shared meal, or the sudden change in how everyone sees him? Let the table hold the question.
