# Red Hood

Red Hood is one of the largest recurring figures in the BLACKSOULS mythos: a girl, a hunter, a mother’s target, an Alice-shaped survivor, and eventually a character who can stand outside the story’s expected ending. Her route crosses *RED HOOD*, *BLACKSOULS I*, *BLACKSOULS II*, and the game’s most direct arguments about memory and authorship.

> **Adults-only warning:** Red Hood’s material includes murder, sexual violence, rape branches, nudity, coercion, demonbeast violence, and disturbing family conflict. The galleries preserve route documentation with descriptive captions rather than explicit prose.

## Appearances in RED HOOD

The earlier *RED HOOD* material establishes the pattern that later games repeat: a child in a fairy tale realizes that the story is hunting her. Lost memories, the Cheshire Cat, Poro, Fenrir, and Grandma turn the familiar path into a sequence of tests about who controls the woods.

## Appearances in BLACKSOULS I

### Meeting Red Hood

In *BLACKSOULS I*, Red Hood’s house is a destination and a trap. The player can buy her, speak with her, enter her kitchen, and find her again in the Holy Forest. Her route oscillates between domestic comedy and the knowledge that the wolf is never far away.

### Violence, killing, and the Basement

## Personality

Red Hood is guarded, observant, and ferociously loyal once trust has been earned. She can be playful and domestic, but those moods never erase the survival instinct underneath. Her anger is not a failure of innocence; it is evidence that she remembers what innocence cost.

The recurring tragedy is that everyone wants Red Hood to perform a role: child, victim, lover, daughter, beast, or heroine. Her strongest scenes are the ones in which she notices the role being imposed and refuses to disappear inside it.

## Design

Red Hood’s visual language shifts from hood, basket, and forest colors to blood-red beast imagery and Alice-like transformations. The silhouette remains readable across every version, which makes the route’s changes feel like alterations to a single person rather than unrelated costumes.
