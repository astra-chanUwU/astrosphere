# VictoriqueMoe and the Personal Anime Web

![VictoriqueMoe](/media/people/victorique-moe/main.jpg)

The web is better when people make places for the things they already care about.

Not every site needs to become a platform. Sometimes a good site begins with a narrower question: what would it feel like to keep a real record of the anime you have watched, the notes you made, and the things you want to return to? Or to give a particular fictional world enough room for its fans to talk, argue, collect, and build together?

That is what I like about [VictoriqueMoe](https://github.com/VictoriqueMoe)'s projects. They are not trying to flatten taste into an endless recommendation feed. They start from the assumption that taste is already a place.

## A watchlist should remember something

[WaifuList](https://waifulist.moe/) is an anime tracker, but calling it only a tracker misses the point. It gives a list a public address, lets people leave notes alongside ratings, and makes room for statistics, discovery, and comparison without making the list feel disposable.

That small difference matters. A watchlist can be a private checklist, but it can also become a diary of what reached you at a particular time. The score is less interesting than the strange route through genres, years, dropped shows, favorites, and unfinished plans. A public list makes that route legible to friends.

My own list lives on [WaifuList](https://waifulist.moe/list/5e1dfaf9-e381-472f-a232-ed6d0ddf2ece?sort=added). It is deliberately incomplete. That is part of the charm: it can keep growing without pretending to be a definitive record of anime itself.

## One world, given enough room

Her newer project, [When They Cry: City of Books](https://whentheycry.social/), takes the opposite direction. Instead of a broad archive of anime taste, it builds a dedicated home around *When They Cry*: a world large enough for theories, mysteries, galleries, writing, and conversations that do not fit neatly into a general-purpose social feed.

This is the old-web instinct I want AstroSphere to keep learning from. A site can be most alive when it has a specific center of gravity. The people who arrive already know why they are there; the system does not need to manufacture urgency for them.

There is a useful contrast here. WaifuList says: keep your path through anime. City of Books says: stay in one world long enough for it to grow roots. Both are more generous than the usual timeline.

## The small web is still happening

Personal projects are often treated as practice for some larger, more acceptable destination. But a well-made site for a particular community is not a lesser version of a giant service. Its limits are its character.

VictoriqueMoe's public work makes a good argument for that. Build something with a point of view. Let its audience be specific. Make the URL worth saving. Then let people return when they have something real to add.

For more, visit [WaifuList](https://waifulist.moe/), [When They Cry: City of Books](https://whentheycry.social/), [GitHub](https://github.com/VictoriqueMoe), or [X](https://x.com/VictoriqueM).
