# THE GHOST IN THE SHELL (2026): Back to the Manga's Strange Joy

![2026 key visual](/media/anime/ghost-in-the-shell-2026/2026-banner.webp)

For a long time, *Ghost in the Shell* on screen has carried a certain expectation: solemn cities, heavy philosophy, a cooler and more controlled Major, and a world that seems to be permanently staring into the rain.

The new *THE GHOST IN THE SHELL* feels different. Science SARU's 2026 series goes back toward Masamune Shirow's manga and, with it, gets permission to be stranger, warmer, more cheerful, and more fun.

That does not make it less cyberpunk. It makes the cyberpunk feel inhabited again.

## The manga was never only one mood

The original manga has always had room for density and absurdity in the same frame. It can jump from a technical detail to a joke, from a hard-edged operation to a character beat, without treating that change in temperature as a mistake.

The 1995 film is a masterpiece. *Stand Alone Complex* is a masterpiece too. But each built a distinct screen-language out of the material: cooler, more procedural, more meditative. The 2026 show has a different invitation. It lets the characters move, tease, improvise, and look like they are enjoying the speed of their own world.

The Major is still dangerous. Section 9 is still navigating a violent networked future. The questions about bodies, ghosts, and information remain. They just arrive in a series with more color in its blood.

## A spectacular return to line and motion

Science SARU's animation is the immediate thing. The show has texture without becoming muddy; its lines feel alive, its machines have weight, and its action is clear enough to be playful. It understands that a future city should have surfaces worth looking at.

The result does not feel like a museum reconstruction of the manga. It feels like someone remembered that Shirow's drawings were allowed to be elaborate, loud, slightly ridiculous, and very beautiful.

![Major and Tachikoma](/media/anime/ghost-in-the-shell-2026/2026-key-visual-tachikoma.webp)

## A franchise with room to change shape

One reason *Ghost in the Shell* has lasted is that it can survive interpretation. The film, *Stand Alone Complex*, games, merchandise, art books, and the original manga do not cancel one another out. They make a larger constellation.

This new series earns its place by not trying to repeat the older versions beat for beat. It is recognizably *Ghost in the Shell*, but its spirit is closer to the manga's restless curiosity than to the franchise's most canonical image of itself.

So far, it has been a pleasure: technically lavish, playful without becoming empty, and confident enough to let a masterpiece be lively.

The [official series site](https://www.theghostintheshell-anime.jp/en/) is worth keeping nearby as the show continues.
