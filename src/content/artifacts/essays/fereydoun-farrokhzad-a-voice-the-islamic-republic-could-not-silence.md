# Fereydoun Farrokhzad: A Voice the Islamic Republic Could Not Silence

On the anniversary of his brutal murder, we remember Fereydoun Farrokhzad—not only as a brilliant artist, but as a fearless Iranian patriot.

A poet, singer, actor, writer, television host, and humanitarian, Farrokhzad represented the confidence and cultural vitality of modern Iran. Through his legendary program *Mikhak-e Noghre’i*—the “Silver Carnation”—he brought laughter, music, elegance, and joy into the homes of millions.

He belonged to an Iran that was modern, ambitious, internationally respected, and proud of its ancient national identity. It was the Iran that the Pahlavi era had helped build: an Iran of progress, education, cultural expression, and national renewal.

After the Islamist seizure of power in 1979, that Iran was attacked by a terrorist regime that claimed to rule in the name of Allah while imprisoning, torturing, murdering, and exiling its own people. The Islamic Republic sought to replace Iranian identity with revolutionary Islamism and to turn the country’s rich culture into an instrument of religious control.

Farrokhzad refused to surrender.

In exile, he became one of the regime’s most courageous critics. He mocked its leaders, condemned its brutality, and continued speaking for a free and dignified Iran. His weapon was his voice—and that was enough to make him dangerous to the mullahs.

In August 1992, Farrokhzad was brutally murdered in his home in Bonn, Germany. His killing was part of the Islamic Republic’s broader campaign of terror against Iranian dissidents abroad. Investigative reporting has documented evidence linking the murder to the regime’s machinery of transnational repression, while German federal police reportedly assessed that it bore the hallmarks of Iranian state terrorism. [Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty](https://www.rferl.org/amp/iran-farrokhzad-chain-murders-investigation-farda/31545582.html)

But the Islamic Republic failed.

It could take his life, but it could not erase his name, his music, his laughter, or his love for Iran. Farrokhzad’s voice continues to reach new generations of Iranians who reject the lies of 1979 and long for the return of a proud, free, and prosperous homeland.

We remember Fereydoun Farrokhzad with love because he loved Iran boldly. We remember him because he refused to surrender his identity to those who wanted to turn Iranian culture into an instrument of religious control. And we remember him because the future he imagined—a free, prosperous, proud Iran—remains worth fighting for.

May his memory be cherished. 🕯

May the days of tyranny come to an end.

May the sun rise again over Iran.

**Javid Shah. پاینده ایران.**
