# Souls on the Banks of the Acheron

Adolf Hirémy-Hirschl’s *Souls on the Banks of the Acheron* (1898) is a monumental Symbolist painting: oil on canvas, more than three metres wide, now held by the Belvedere in Vienna. Its subject comes from Greek mythology. Acheron—the river of grief—is one of the thresholds of the underworld, where the dead wait to be carried onward.

The painting gives that waiting a physical, almost unbearable density. Pale bodies press together at the water’s edge, reaching, collapsing, and turning toward the winged figure who enters from the right. He can be read as Hermes Psychopompos, the guide of souls. His calm movement cuts through the crowd’s panic, while the dark landscape seems to absorb both the people and the light.

But the image does not need a single explanation to hold its power. Look at where the figures gather, where the blue and white cloth catch your eye, and how the guide’s direction changes the whole space. Stay with the faces and gestures for a moment. The painting may be about death, judgment, helplessness, or simply the experience of being carried toward something unknown. See what it becomes for you.
