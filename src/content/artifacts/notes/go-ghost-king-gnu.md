# GO GHOST: A New Signal for Ghost in the Shell

The new *Ghost in the Shell* already has a more vivid, playful pulse than the franchise's usual screen mood. King Gnu's **GO GHOST** is a good match for that change: fast, electronic, restless, and built to make the opening feel like a transmission rather than a thesis statement.

The song is the opening theme for the current TV anime, and its music video takes an unusually direct route through that relationship. It cuts animated footage from the series to the track instead of treating the song and show as separate promotions. The result is less like a detached tie-in and more like a second opening sequence—one that lets the new show’s color, motion, and strange cheerfulness run loose.

## A theme that moves with the show

*Ghost in the Shell* music has often been asked to carry atmosphere: ritual, distance, melancholy, surveillance, rain. **GO GHOST** comes at the material from another angle. It is impatient. It has the energy of a crowded future city in motion, where every signal is competing to arrive first.

That makes sense for this version of the series. The anime is closer to the manga’s room for humour and momentum, not only its most solemn questions. A bright opening does not make the ghost less strange; it gives the network another rhythm.

The official announcement notes that the digital release landed on July 29 and that the music video premiered on August 4. It also describes the video as King Gnu’s first use of anime footage edited specifically around one of their songs.
